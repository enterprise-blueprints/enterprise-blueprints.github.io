Capability Catalog

Overview

The Enterprise Capability Map describes business capabilities that consume and compose shared enterprise capabilities. This catalog gives those capabilities a first, concrete form.

Each entry states the capability’s responsibility and boundaries, its operations at a logical level, the entities it acts on, and the policy areas that govern it. It also lists the signs that a capability is missing or duplicated, and questions to ask before building a new one. Those support the habit of searching before building.

Use cases and missions refer to these capabilities by ID. Activities name the capabilities they use, requirements name the capabilities that could fulfill them, and typical systems name the capabilities they provide today. Each entry shows where the library uses it.

How to read this catalog

Operations use names such as Notification.send to describe a responsibility, not an interface. An implementation may provide one through an API, an event, a workflow, or an agent tool. Inputs, outputs, failure behavior, and service levels belong in fuller capability contracts, which later artifacts will develop.

The catalog is a starting point, not a complete inventory, and it doesn’t imply one central platform. An organization may share a capability across its programs, run separate implementations that agree on a contract, or keep a specialized capability where its work requires one.

Case Management appears as a business capability, as it does in the capability map. Reporting appears among the shared capability examples to support oversight and statistics.

The data lives in _data/capabilities/, one file per capability. Tools can read the whole catalog as JSON at /capabilities/index.json. The rest of this page is generated from that data.

Business capabilities

What the enterprise does. Programs own the rules of their work

Case Management

Keep a matter's people, information, tasks, decisions, and history together from receipt to closure, with one accountable owner at a time.

Boundaries: Each program defines its own case types, rules, and completion criteria; Case Management gives them a common model to build on. It links to documents, people, and tasks held by other capabilities rather than owning them.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Case.createOpen a case from a matter, with its source and case type.source, matter details, case typecase ID
Case.getReturn the view of a case that the requester may see.case ID, purposeauthorized case view
Case.assignGive the case an accountable owner, and record the change.case ID, ownerassignment
Case.linkLink related cases while each keeps its own history.case IDs, relationshiplink
Case.recordDecisionRecord a decision with its reasons, evidence, and decision-maker.case ID, decision, reasons, evidencedecision ID
Case.closeRecord the outcome and close the case, keeping its history.case ID, outcomeclosed case

Acts on: case, participant, interaction, task, assignment, decision
Policy: authorization, privacy, audit, records
Depends on: Person Information, Document Management, Workflow, Search

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Each program keeps its own case tracker or spreadsheet. Staff re-key the same matter into more than one system. Nobody can say who owns a case after it changes hands.

Questions to ask: Which systems hold cases, or records that work like cases, today? Do different teams mean different things by a case?

Shared enterprise capabilities

Reusable actions that experiences, workflows, and agents use through a clear contract.

Address Validation

Check that an address exists and is deliverable, and return it in a standard form.

Boundaries: It assesses addresses; it doesn't decide which address is a person's or organization's address of record.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Address.validateCheck and standardize an address.addressstandardized address, validation result

Acts on: address
Policy: data governance

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Notices come back undeliverable. Each form validates addresses differently, or not at all.

Questions to ask: How often are notices returned as undeliverable?

Approval

Route a decision to a person with the authority to make it, and record the decision, the reasons, and who made it.

Boundaries: The business program establishes who has authority for each decision; Approval enforces and records it. An agent may prepare a recommendation, but a consequential decision needs an authorized person.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Approval.requestAsk for a decision from someone with the authority to make it.subject, decision needed, criteria, supporting evidencerequest ID
Approval.decideRecord an approver's decision and reasons.request ID, outcome, reasonsdecision ID
Approval.getStatusReport where a request stands and who it is waiting on.request IDstatus

Acts on: decision
Policy: authorization, audit, ai policy
Depends on: Workflow, Identity

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Approvals happen by email reply. The same decision goes up two or three levels. Nobody can show who approved something, or when.

Questions to ask: Which decisions need approval, and who may give it? Where do requests most often wait for a decision?

Used in the library:

  • Public Sector Case Management: 1 activity, 2 template requirements

Document Management

Store, classify, version, and retrieve documents and files, and apply the records rules that govern them.

Boundaries: It knows how a document is classified, secured, retained, and retrieved. The consuming process decides which documents belong to its work and which one is the official version.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Document.storeStore a document with its classification and the record it belongs to.file, classification, related recorddocument ID
Document.getReturn a document the requester may see, and record the retrieval.document ID, purposedocument
Document.classifySet or change a document's classification and access restrictions.document ID, classificationclassification
Document.applyRetentionApply a records schedule, and hold, transfer, or dispose of the document as it allows.document ID, records scheduleretention status

Acts on: document
Policy: records, privacy, security, audit, accessibility
Depends on: Identity

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Case files live on shared drives and personal laptops. Nobody is sure which copy of a document is the official one. Retention is applied by hand, or not at all.

Questions to ask: Where do documents for this work live today, and who can change them? Which records schedules apply, and who applies them?

Identity

Establish who or what is acting, whether staff, a member of the public, a partner, or an agent, and with what level of assurance.

Boundaries: Identity says who is acting. What they may do is an authorization decision, made under enterprise policy using that identity.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Identity.authenticateSign a person, system, or agent in.credentialsauthenticated identity, assurance level
Identity.verifyCheck evidence that a person is who they claim to be.person details, evidenceverification result, assurance level
Identity.getAttributesReturn the attributes of an identity that a consumer may use, such as role and organization.identity, purposeattributes

Acts on: person, organization
Policy: identity, security, privacy, audit

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Applications keep their own user accounts and passwords. External partners share accounts, or can't get one. Accounts stay active after people change jobs.

Questions to ask: How do staff, the public, and partners sign in today, and to how many systems? Which actions need a higher level of identity assurance?

Used in the library:

  • Public Sector Case Management: 1 activity, 2 template requirements
  • Inspector General Investigations: 3 mission requirements; typically provided today by Identity provider (PIV and single sign-on)

Notification

Deliver approved messages to people through their preferred channels, and report whether each one was delivered.

Boundaries: The consuming process decides what to say and to whom; Notification delivers it. The process keeps the message in its own record when the message is part of that record.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Notification.sendSend an approved message to a recipient.recipient, approved message, delivery preferencedelivery ID
Notification.getStatusReport whether a message was delivered, opened, or failed.delivery IDdelivery status

Acts on: person, organization, interaction
Policy: privacy, records, accessibility
Depends on: Person Information

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Several systems each send their own email. Nobody can say whether a notice was delivered. People can't choose how they're contacted.

Questions to ask: Which systems send messages to staff, the public, or partners today? Do any notices have legal or records requirements?

Organization Information

Provide an authorized view of an organization, such as a company, vendor, grantee, or agency, from the authoritative records, and match new information to it.

Boundaries: It reads and matches; it changes an authoritative record only where its owner has agreed. Registration and vendor management processes own their own records.

Organization.getReturn the view of an organization that the requester may see.organization ID, purposeauthorized organization view
Organization.matchSay whether new details belong to an existing organization.organization detailsmatch, confidence

Acts on: organization, address
Policy: authorization, audit, data governance

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Vendors and grantees are recorded separately in each program. Nobody can list every case involving the same company.

Questions to ask: Which system is authoritative for vendors, grantees, and other organizations?

Payment

Take and make payments, and report their status, under the enterprise's financial controls.

Boundaries: The financial system of record keeps the ledger. Programs decide what is owed or payable; Payment carries it out and reports back.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns

Acts on: payment, person, organization

Questions to ask: Does this work take or make payments, and through which systems?

Person Information

Provide an authorized view of a person from the authoritative records, and match new information to a person the enterprise already knows.

Boundaries: It reads and matches; it changes an authoritative record only where its owner has agreed. Each program still decides what it needs to know about a person and why.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Person.lookupFind people who match identifying details.identifying details, purposecandidate matches
Person.getReturn the view of a person that the requester may see for a stated purpose.person ID, purposeauthorized person view
Person.matchSay whether new details belong to an existing person, and how confident the match is.person detailsmatch, confidence

Acts on: person, address
Policy: privacy, authorization, audit, data governance

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: The same person has different identifiers in different systems. Staff search several systems to find someone's current details.

Questions to ask: Which system is authoritative for each kind of person: employees, members of the public, partners? How do you tell whether two records describe the same person?

Reporting

Provide measures and views of the work from authoritative data, such as workload, age, status, and results, with consistent definitions.

Boundaries: It reports from the systems of record and doesn't become another source of truth. Each measure has an owner and an agreed definition.
Note: Not among the capability map's examples. Added because oversight and statistics need it in almost every program.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Report.getReturn a measure or view, filtered to what the requester may see.measure, filtersresults
Report.scheduleProduce a report on a schedule and deliver it.measure, filters, schedule, recipientsschedule ID

Acts on: case, task, assignment, decision
Policy: authorization, privacy, data governance
Depends on: Identity

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Statistics are reconciled by hand in spreadsheets. Each office counts the same thing differently. Supervisors can't see workload without asking.

Questions to ask: How long does it take to answer a question like “how many cases did we close last year?” Who defines each measure, and who checks it?

Boundaries: It finds and points to information; the systems of record stay authoritative. Results respect each record's access rules.

Workflow

Coordinate tasks across people and systems: who does what, by when, and what happens when it's late.

Boundaries: It moves work and tracks it. Who has authority to decide belongs to Approval and enterprise policy, and the rules of each process belong to the program that owns it.

OperationWhat it doesAcceptsReturns
Task.createCreate a task with an assignee and a due date.work, assignee, due date, related recordtask ID
Task.assignAssign or reassign a task, keeping its history.task ID, assigneeassignment
Task.completeRecord a task's result.task ID, resultcompleted task
Task.escalateRaise an overdue or stalled task to the right person.task ID, reasonescalation

Acts on: task, assignment
Policy: authorization, audit
Depends on: Notification, Identity

Signs it’s missing or duplicated: Work is tracked in email, personal lists, and spreadsheets. Overdue work turns up in meetings instead of being flagged. Each application has its own task list.

Questions to ask: How does someone know what they need to do today, and what is overdue? What happens when a task waits on someone outside the team?
