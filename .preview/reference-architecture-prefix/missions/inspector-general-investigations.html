Inspector General Investigations

Overview

An Office of Inspector General (OIG) investigates fraud, waste, abuse, and misconduct in the programs and operations of its agency. Investigations start from complaints, hotline submissions, referrals, and the office’s own analysis. They end in findings that support criminal, civil, or administrative action.

This mission model describes what shapes how well that work goes. It connects the conditions that enable or inhibit the work, the behaviors of investigators and supervisors, the operational outcomes those behaviors produce, and the mission outcomes the public cares about.

Use it in discovery to walk a customer through how their investigations operate today. Ask about each factor, and note whether it is a strength or a problem. The relationships then point to the biggest opportunities for improvement and to the outcomes and metrics worth committing to.

How to read this model

Every relationship is a proposition to test with a customer, not an established fact about any agency. Most are marked as hypotheses. Evidence from real engagements, research, and performance data should strengthen, refine, or contradict them over time.

The model includes interventions of every kind. A technology solution can change some factors, such as visibility into stalled requests. Others, such as closure targets or staffing, depend on policy, process, and people. Those are out of scope for a technology solution, but they belong in the conversation.

The model also describes how the work flows, in two processes: complaint intake and triage, and investigations. Both specialize the Public Sector Case Management process. Each factor names the activities where it shows up, and each technology intervention names the requirements that would deliver it. Those requirements add to, and in places specialize, the use case’s template requirements.

The feedback loops matter most. The closure pressure loop suggests that pushing for more closed cases may grow the backlog, which is the opposite of what a closure target intends.

The model’s data lives in _data/missions/inspector-general-investigations.yml. Tools can read every mission model as JSON at /missions/index.json. The rest of this page is generated from that data.

Feedback loops

Closure pressure grows the backlog

Reinforcing loop: Investigator caseload → Pressure to close cases → Reviews and documentation are rushed → Quality deficiencies and rework → Investigator caseload

A heavy caseload creates pressure to close cases. Rushed reviews and documentation cause rework, which keeps cases open and adds to the caseload. Pushing for more closures may make the backlog worse.

Watch for: Proposals that make closure counts the main measure of success.

Success brings more work

Balancing loop: Timely, thorough, and defensible investigations support accountability → Employees and the public trust that reported wrongdoing is addressed → Volume of incoming allegations → Case backlog → Investigator caseload → Investigations progress without avoidable delay → Timely, thorough, and defensible investigations support accountability

Visible results build trust, which brings more allegations. Without more capacity, the extra work slows investigations and limits further gains.

Watch for: Forecasts that assume allegation volume stays flat after improvements.
